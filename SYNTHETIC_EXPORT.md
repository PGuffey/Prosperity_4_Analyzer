# Synthetic quotes and backtester export

## Quote customization

Customize **bids, asks and available size**, not executed
trades. On Synthetic, select or generate a dataset and use the top **Bids, asks &
export** button. Its dialog is separate from the library and supports Escape/Close.

Set distances below/above each current mid and bid/ask sizes, or supply exact
quotes with `snapshot,bid_price,bid_size,ask_price,ask_size` CSV columns. CSV rows
patch only the listed zero-based playback indexes; repeated indexes, crossed quotes
and non-positive prices/sizes are rejected. Formula mode keeps missing mid rows
unchanged; CSV can explicitly supply their quotes. Bids round down and asks up.
Mid is recalculated from the resulting quotes; asymmetric distances may move it.

**Create copy with quotes** saves and selects a new scenario. Original data is
unchanged, and any legacy execution tape is excluded from the new copy. Preview
the first eight quote rows, then export the selected copy. Existing legacy datasets
are not deleted; exporting one directly retains its old tape and shows a warning.

No trade executions are generated by quote customization. Backend compatibility
endpoints for older trade-bearing datasets remain available, but are not part of
the current quote editor.

## Legacy trade-bearing datasets

Older datasets may include optional market trade tapes. They remain readable and
can be exported directly. The legacy API supports these operations (not UI controls):

- **Generate simple trade flow:** set trade probability per snapshot, probability
  of a buy at the ask rather than a sell at the bid, maximum quantity, and seed.
  There is at most one print per snapshot. Quantity is sampled uniformly from
  one to the smaller of your limit and displayed depth. Missing sides are skipped.
- **Use my CSV:** upload or paste prints with `snapshot,price,quantity` and optional
  `buyer,seller`. Snapshot is the zero-based stored playback row, not a timestamp.
  This avoids ambiguity after day shuffling. Prices/quantities are positive whole
  numbers. Multiple prints per snapshot are allowed. Custom prints are not shifted
  or capped to fit the synthetic book. Limit: 5 MB / 100,000 prints.
- **Create copy with trades:** creates a separate library item. The source scenario
  is unchanged. The manifest records generated/custom origin, parent run and
  generator settings; old price-only runs remain usable.
- **Export backtester ZIP:** exports the selected copy's tape and price books.
  A price-only item exports an empty tape. Preview shows the first 100 prints.

These are market prints consumed by a backtester, not orders or fills from your
own trader. Imported own-trade logs must be deliberately converted if you want
to use them as a market-flow scenario; that changes their meaning.

## Export contract and limitations

The ZIP contains `roundN/prices_round_N_day_0.csv`,
`roundN/trades_round_N_day_0.csv`, `snapshot_map.csv`, `manifest.json` and a README.
All source days are flattened into **one artificial playback day 0** in stored
order, with timestamp `snapshot * 100`. The mapping retains original day/time.
This is not an assertion that competition events last one day. Gaps in source
time are not reproduced as idle intervals.

Books contain level 1 only; levels 2/3 are blank, not copied from an untransformed
market. Bids round down and asks round up to whole price ticks; volumes round down.
Exported mid is recomputed from available rounded quotes. A snapshot without a
usable book, or with a crossed book, blocks export with a readable error. No
conversion observations, terminal fair values or extra products are invented.

Use a trader that supports the selected single product and does not require
missing observation fields. The simple trade generator is reproducible but does
not model queue priority, correlated bot behavior or realistic fill probability.
Its backtest results answer a scenario question, not a calibrated profitability
estimate. There is no strategy execution inside PMA.

## What the backtester changed in our design

The supplied [CLI](https://github.com/kevin-fu1/imc-prosperity-4-backtester/blob/main/prosperity4bt/__main__.py)
supports a custom data directory. Its
[reader](https://github.com/kevin-fu1/imc-prosperity-4-backtester/blob/main/prosperity4bt/tools/data_reader.py)
uses per-round price/trade CSV files with semicolon-separated fields. Its
[price model](https://github.com/kevin-fu1/imc-prosperity-4-backtester/blob/main/prosperity4bt/models/input.py)
parses book prices and volumes as integers. These are the compatibility changes
implemented here (sources reviewed 2026-09-12).

After extracting the ZIP into a separate folder, from an installed backtester:

```powershell
python -m prosperity4bt path/to/trader.py 1-0 --data path/to/extracted --no-vis
```

Replace `1` with the exported round. Use the explicit artificial day 0 because
the reader's default available-day list may exclude it. PMA does not modify the
backtester, its bundled competition data, or its matching/settlement behavior.

CSV field order, integer parsing, file names, tape/time mapping and ZIP contents
are covered by PMA tests. A complete run of your trader in the external backtester
has **not** been verified. Before relying on results, smoke-test that
trader, check its product/observation requirements and review the chosen backtester
version's matching and settlement assumptions. The README is not official evidence
about the length or terminal value rules of competition testing events.
